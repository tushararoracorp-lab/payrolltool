A full and final (F&F) settlement brings together everything owed to an employee who leaves, and everything recovered from them, in one statement. Since 21 November 2025 the gratuity part runs under the Code on Social Security, 2020, which replaced the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972. That changed who qualifies, which wages count and how the settlement is timed. This guide walks through each component the way a payroll team builds it.

## What goes into the settlement

**Payable:** salary for the days worked in the final month, gratuity where the employee qualifies, leave encashment, and any pending dues such as bonus or reimbursements.

**Recoverable:** notice period shortfall, where the employee did not serve the notice their contract requires, and any amounts the employee owes, such as advances or unreturned company property.

## Payment timing: two working days

Under section 17(2) of the Code on Wages, 2019, wages due to an employee who resigns, is removed, dismissed or retrenched must be paid within two working days of leaving. Plan the F&F workflow around that date, not around the next monthly payroll run.

## Gratuity: who qualifies

**Permanent employees** qualify after five years of continuous service. A year of continuous service means 240 days worked in twelve months, or 190 days where the establishment works fewer than six days a week (section 54 of the Code).

**Fixed-term employees** qualify after one year of service under the contract, counted from the start of the contract, as the Ministry of Labour and Employment's FAQs of 16 March 2026 confirm. Fixed-term employment covers people the employer engages directly. An 11-month contract does not reach the one-year mark.

**Contract labour** engaged through a contractor follows the five-year rule, and the contractor pays the gratuity.

**Death or disablement:** no minimum service applies. Gratuity is paid to the nominee or legal heir in the case of death.

## Gratuity: the formula

**Gratuity = last drawn wages × 15 ÷ 26 × completed years of service**

The 15 ÷ 26 gives fifteen days' wages for each year, taking a month as 26 working days. A final part-year of more than six months counts as a full year: 6 years 8 months is 7 years, while 6 years 5 months is 6 years.

The maximum payable is Rs. 20 lakh, the amount notified by the Central Government.

## Gratuity: which wages count

"Last drawn wages" now means wages as defined in the Labour Codes: basic pay, dearness allowance, retaining allowance and any other pay the Code does not exclude. For employees who leave on or after 21 November 2025, gratuity is paid on these wages.

The 50% rule applies here too. If the components excluded from wages, such as HRA, conveyance and statutory bonus, add up to more than half of total remuneration, the excess over half is added back into wages. A salary structure with a deliberately low basic therefore produces a higher gratuity wage than the basic line on the payslip suggests. Our guide to the [Code on Wages 50% rule](/blog/code-on-wages-50-percent-rule) works through an example.

## Leave encashment

Under the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020, a worker can carry forward up to 30 days of leave, and on leaving is entitled to wages in lieu of the leave to their credit. "Wages" here carries the same Labour Code definition as for gratuity, including the 50% rule. For employees outside the definition of worker, the company's leave policy decides which leave types are encashable and how many days can be carried.

The formula is **wages ÷ divisor × leave days**, where the divisor (30, 26 or the calendar days of the month) is the one your leave policy states.

## Notice period shortfall

If the contract requires 60 days' notice and the employee serves 25, the 35-day shortfall is recovered from the settlement. The appointment letter or policy states whether the recovery is calculated on wages or on gross salary, and the two can differ by a large amount, so apply exactly what the letter says.

## Tax treatment

Both exemptions apply under the old and the new regime.

**Gratuity** is exempt under section 19(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly section 10(10)), up to the least of the actual gratuity, the formula amount and Rs. 20 lakh. The Rs. 20 lakh is a lifetime limit across all employers.

**Leave encashment** at retirement or resignation is exempt for non-government employees up to Rs. 25 lakh (formerly section 10(10AA)), also a lifetime limit across employers. Any amount above the exempt limit is taxed as salary in the year it is paid.

## A complete worked example

An employee resigns after 6 years 8 months. Last drawn wages are Rs. 55,000 a month, gross salary is Rs. 65,000, and the excluded components stay under half of remuneration, so no add-back applies. The employee has 30 days of earned leave, served 25 of a required 60 days' notice, has a pending bonus of Rs. 5,000 and owes Rs. 2,000 for an unreturned device.

**Gratuity:** service counts as 7 years. Rs. 55,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 7 = **Rs. 2,22,115**

**Leave encashment** (policy divisor 30): Rs. 55,000 ÷ 30 × 30 = **Rs. 55,000**

**Notice shortfall** (policy basis: gross): Rs. 65,000 ÷ 30 × 35 = **Rs. 75,833**, recovered

**Pending bonus:** Rs. 5,000

**Device recovery:** Rs. 2,000, recovered

**Net settlement** = 2,22,115 + 55,000 + 5,000 − 75,833 − 2,000 = **Rs. 2,04,282**, plus salary for the days worked in the final month, which is processed with its usual statutory deductions.

Both the gratuity and the leave encashment fall within their exempt limits here, so neither adds to the employee's taxable salary.

**A fixed-term example:** a one-year contract on last drawn wages of Rs. 30,000. Rs. 30,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 1 = **Rs. 17,308**.

## Common mistakes

**Applying the five-year rule to fixed-term employees.** Since 21 November 2025 they qualify after one year under the contract.

**Taking the basic line from the payslip as wages.** Wages under the Code include DA, retaining allowance and any pay not on the excluded list, and the 50% test can add more. Gratuity and leave encashment both use this figure.

**Rounding the final part-year the wrong way.** Only a part-year of more than six months rounds up.

**Treating Rs. 20 lakh as a per-employer limit for tax.** The exemption is a lifetime total.

**Waiting for the next payroll cycle.** Wages on exit are due within two working days.
