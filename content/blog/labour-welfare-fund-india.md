The Labour Welfare Fund (LWF) is a small statutory contribution from employees and employers, and in some states from the State Government too, that pays for welfare schemes for workers: education help for workers' children, medical help, housing, training and recreation. Each state's Labour Welfare Board runs its own fund.

There is no central LWF law. Each state that collects it has its own Act, its own amounts, its own ratio between the employee and the employer, and its own cycle. That is why LWF is easy to get wrong in a multi-state payroll even though the amounts are small.

## Which states collect it

Sixteen states and union territories run a Labour Welfare Fund that payroll has to deduct: Andhra Pradesh, Chandigarh, Chhattisgarh, Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Karnataka, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Punjab, Tamil Nadu, Telangana and West Bengal.

Large states including Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Bihar and Jharkhand have no LWF deduction. As with professional tax, what matters is the state where the employee works, not where the company is registered.

## Contributions by state

- **Andhra Pradesh**: employee Rs. 30 a year, employer Rs. 70 a year (annual)
- **Chandigarh**: employee Rs. 5 a month, employer Rs. 20 a month (follows Punjab)
- **Chhattisgarh**: employee Rs. 15 per half-year, employer Rs. 45 per half-year (half-yearly)
- **Delhi**: employee Rs. 0.75, employer Rs. 2.25 (half-yearly)
- **Goa**: employee Rs. 60 per half-year, employer Rs. 180 per half-year (half-yearly)
- **Gujarat**: employee Rs. 6 per half-year, employer Rs. 12 per half-year (half-yearly)
- **Haryana**: employee 0.2% of wages, up to Rs. 35 a month, employer Twice the employee's share, up to Rs. 70 a month (monthly)
- **Karnataka**: employee Rs. 50 a year, employer Rs. 100 a year (annual)
- **Kerala (shops and commercial establishments)**: employee Rs. 50 a month, employer Rs. 50 a month (monthly)
- **Madhya Pradesh**: employee Rs. 10 per half-year, employer Rs. 50 per half-year (half-yearly)
- **Maharashtra**: employee Rs. 25 per half-year, employer Rs. 75 per half-year (half-yearly)
- **Odisha**: employee Rs. 10 per half-year, employer Rs. 20 per half-year (half-yearly)
- **Punjab**: employee Rs. 5 a month, employer Rs. 20 a month (monthly contribution)
- **Tamil Nadu**: employee Rs. 20 a year, employer Rs. 40 a year (annual)
- **Telangana**: employee Rs. 2 a year, employer Rs. 5 a year (annual)
- **West Bengal**: employee Rs. 3 per half-year, employer Rs. 30 per half-year (half-yearly)

In Delhi the State Government adds Rs. 1.50 for each employee. In Goa it adds Rs. 240 a year. Those government shares never pass through payroll.

## There is no standard ratio

The employer's share is a different multiple of the employee's share in almost every state:

- **1 : 1** in Kerala's shops fund
- **1 : 2** in Gujarat, Haryana, Karnataka, Odisha and Tamil Nadu
- **1 : 3** in Chhattisgarh, Delhi, Goa and Maharashtra
- **1 : 4** in Punjab and Chandigarh
- **1 : 5** in Madhya Pradesh
- **1 : 10** in West Bengal
- **2 : 5** in Telangana and **3 : 7** in Andhra Pradesh, neither of them a whole multiple

So a payroll set-up that takes the employee amount and multiplies it to get the employer amount will be wrong in most states. Hold both amounts as separate figures for each state.

## The cycles and due dates

**Half-yearly states** (Chhattisgarh, Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, West Bengal) usually deduct from the June and December salaries and deposit by 15 July and 15 January. Goa's deposit dates are 31 July and 31 January.

**Annual states** deduct once, from the December salary. Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and Telangana deposit by 31 January; Karnataka by 15 January.

**Monthly states** deduct every month. Haryana and Kerala's shops fund are paid monthly; Kerala's contributions are due by the 5th of the following month.

## Recent changes to check

- **Madhya Pradesh** raised the contributions to Rs. 10 from the employee and Rs. 50 from the employer per half-year.
- **Haryana** sets the employee share at 0.2 per cent of wages with a ceiling, Rs. 35 a month from 1 January 2026 (Rs. 34 before), and lets employers deposit monthly online.
- **Maharashtra** moved to flat amounts of Rs. 25 and Rs. 75 per half-year from June 2024 and dropped the old Rs. 3,000 wage slab. Late payment carries interest of 1.5 per cent a month for the first three months and 2 per cent after that.
- **West Bengal** set the employer share at Rs. 30 per half-year from 1 January 2024 and requires electronic payment.
- **Tamil Nadu** raised the amounts to Rs. 20 and Rs. 40 a year.
- **Goa's** amounts come from the 2015 amendment to its Act; older tables showing Rs. 10 and Rs. 30 are out of date.

## Common mistakes

**Recovering the employer's share from wages.** The employer's contribution is a cost to the employer. Deducting it from the employee is an unauthorised deduction.

**Deducting every month in a half-yearly or annual state.** The amounts are set per half-year or per year. A monthly deduction in Maharashtra or Tamil Nadu takes far too much.

**Missing the months nothing happens.** In half-yearly and annual states, payroll does nothing for five or eleven months and then has to remember. Put the deduction months and deposit dates in the compliance calendar.

**Deducting from excluded staff.** Several states exclude managerial and supervisory staff above a salary limit. Chhattisgarh covers employees up to Rs. 10,000 a month in non-managerial roles; Delhi excludes managerial and supervisory staff above Rs. 2,500 a month.

**Copying one state's figures to another.** Chhattisgarh was formed from Madhya Pradesh and Telangana from Andhra Pradesh, but their LWF amounts are different. Take each state's figures from its own notification.

**Losing portal access.** West Bengal requires electronic payment and most boards now take money online. Keep the login on an official email address and mobile number so a staff change does not stop the payment.

## Key takeaway

The Labour Welfare Fund is small, but it is sixteen separate laws with sixteen sets of amounts, ratios and dates. Set it up per state of work, keep the employee and employer amounts as separate figures, and give the half-yearly and annual deductions their own entries in the compliance calendar.
