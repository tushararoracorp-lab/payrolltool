**Quick answer:** The statutory EPF wage ceiling is now **Rs 25,000 per month**, up from Rs 15,000, effective **17 September 2026** (Notification S.O. 5109(E)). Any employee drawing wages up to Rs 25,000 is now within mandatory EPF, EPS and EDLI coverage. If you run payroll, the immediate task is the September 2026 ECR - it has to be filed as a single return split across the old and new ceiling, due **15 October 2026**.

## At a glance: what actually changed

**Mandatory coverage wage ceiling** - Rs 15,000/month before 17 Sept 2026 → Rs 25,000/month from 17 Sept 2026.

**Max employer EPS contribution (8.33%)** - ~Rs 1,250/month before → ~Rs 2,083/month from 17 Sept 2026.

**Max employer EPF contribution (3.67%)** - ~Rs 550/month before → ~Rs 917/month from 17 Sept 2026.

**Government's own EPS contribution (1.16%)** - Capped at Rs 15,000 wages before, and **unchanged** from 17 Sept 2026 - still capped at Rs 15,000 wages, max ~Rs 174/month.

**Employees newly brought into mandatory coverage** - Est. **51 lakh** nationally, from 17 Sept 2026.

## Why now

This wasn't a routine revision. The Supreme Court, in an order dated **5 January 2026** (Bench of Justices J.K. Maheshwari and Atul S. Chandurkar), directed the Centre to decide on revising the ceiling - unchanged for over a decade - within four months. The government's first response, a notification dated 29 May 2026 (S.O. 2702(E)), actually left the ceiling untouched at Rs 15,000. It was only after the Expenditure Finance Committee's recommendation on 16 June 2026 and Union Cabinet approval on 16 September 2026 that the increase to Rs 25,000 was formally notified the next day, superseding the May notification.

The government's own estimate: **over 51 lakh additional employees** brought into mandatory EPFO coverage, at an annual outgo of roughly **Rs 11,339 crore** (up from Rs 10,250 crore currently budgeted) - about **Rs 56,696 crore over five years**.

## What this changes for payroll

**Mandatory coverage widens.** Anyone earning up to Rs 25,000/month is now mandatorily covered - previously, coverage above Rs 15,000 required an employer-employee agreement. If you have employees in the Rs 15,001-25,000 band who weren't contributing before, they need to be identified and enrolled - no separate application is required from the employee; enrolment is the employer's statutory responsibility.

**EPS contribution ceiling moves too.** Employer EPS contribution (8.33%) now applies up to Rs 25,000 - a maximum of roughly Rs 2,083/month, up from Rs 1,250. EPS membership for the newly-covered band is automatic, not optional.

**The Government's own contribution doesn't move.** A detail that's easy to miss: the Government's EPS contribution stays capped at 1.16% of wages up to Rs 15,000 - roughly Rs 174/month per member, unchanged. This has no bearing on a member's own pension, which still depends on pensionable wages and pensionable service.

**EDLI cover does *not* rise to Rs 10.5 lakh, despite what the math suggests.** With the wage-linked component now calculated up to Rs 25,000, the arithmetic (35 x average monthly wage, plus 50% of average PF balance) can mathematically exceed Rs 10 lakh. But the maximum EDLI assurance benefit stays capped at **Rs 7 lakh** under the current EDLI Scheme, 2026 - that's not changing with this notification.

**Nothing changes for employees already contributing above the old ceiling.** If your organisation was already computing PF on actual wages above Rs 15,000, existing arrangements continue to be governed by the applicable statutory provisions - the enhancement doesn't force a reduction, and it doesn't require restructuring what's already compliant.

**Employer cost is a real, recurring increase - partly offsettable.** The additional cost is a predictable 12% matching contribution on the newly-covered band. Employers may be able to partly offset this through **PMVBRY**, which offers an incentive of up to **Rs 3,000/month per additional employee** created - MSMEs get no separate rate but qualify for the same PMVBRY offset.

## How to file the September 2026 ECR (the part everyone's asking about)

This is a **single ECR for September**, not two separate filings - but the contribution has to be calculated across two periods within the month, since the ceiling changed mid-cycle:

- **Period 1: 1-16 September** - contribution calculated against the old Rs 15,000 ceiling
- **Period 2: 17-30 September** - contribution calculated against the new Rs 25,000 ceiling

**Worked example - employee with EPF wages of Rs 20,000/month, already an EPF member but not yet an EPS member (a very common scenario for anyone above the old Rs 15,000 ceiling):**

**Period 1 (1-16 Sept, 16 days):**
- Proportionate wages: Rs 10,666.67 (Rs 20,000 x 16/30)
- EPS wages: Nil - not yet an EPS member
- Employee EPF (12%): on Rs 10,666.67
- Employer split: 12% entirely to EPF

**Period 2 (17-30 Sept, 14 days):**
- Proportionate wages: Rs 9,333.33 (Rs 20,000 x 14/30)
- EPS wages: Rs 9,333.33 - EPS membership starts here
- Employee EPF (12%): on Rs 9,333.33
- Employer split: 3.67% EPF + 8.33% EPS

Total statutory remittance for the month works out to roughly **Rs 5,000** across employee and employer shares combined for this example - the exact figures depend on which of the three common scenarios applies to a given employee (already excluded, already an EPF-only member becoming EPS-eligible, or already contributing at the old Rs 15,000 cap).

**If payroll couldn't deduct the additional employee share in time:** you're permitted to recover it in the October payroll cycle for take-home computation purposes, without needing prior approval from the Inspector-cum-Facilitator. That flexibility applies only to the *internal payroll deduction* - the ECR itself, and the full statutory remittance (both shares), still has to be filed and paid by **15 October 2026**.

## FAQs

**Is the EPF wage ceiling really Rs 25,000 now, not Rs 15,000?**

Yes. Effective 17 September 2026, under Notification S.O. 5109(E), issued under Section 2(89) of the Code on Social Security, 2020.

**Do employers need to file two ECRs for September 2026?**

No - one ECR for the wage month, with contributions calculated separately for the 1-16 September and 17-30 September periods.

**When is the September 2026 ECR due?**

15 October 2026, same as the standard monthly deadline.

**Will take-home salary go down because of this?**

The additional PF deduction moves money from take-home into the employee's own PF account - it's not lost, it's matched by the employer, earns interest, and builds toward pension and insurance cover.

**Does this apply to employees already earning above Rs 25,000?**

Only up to the new ceiling. Wages above Rs 25,000 are treated the same way wages above Rs 15,000 were before, unless the employee is already covered on higher wages under an existing arrangement.

**Will the EDLI maximum benefit increase along with the ceiling?**

No - it stays at Rs 7 lakh under the current EDLI Scheme, 2026, even though the wage-linked calculation can mathematically exceed that figure.

## Key takeaway

The EPF wage ceiling is Rs 25,000 as of 17 September 2026. The immediate compliance task isn't the ceiling itself - it's getting the September ECR right, split correctly across the two periods, filed by 15 October. If you have employees between Rs 15,001 and Rs 25,000 who weren't previously covered, get them identified and enrolled now, not at your next audit.
