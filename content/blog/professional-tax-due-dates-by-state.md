Professional tax is deducted every month in most states, but it is not paid on the same day everywhere. Each state's Act and rules set their own due date, and several states do not work monthly at all: some are half-yearly, some annual, and one is an annual tax paid quarterly. For an employer in more than one state, the due date is the part of professional tax most likely to be missed, because the amount is small and nothing in payroll fails when a payment is late.

This calendar lists the due date for tax deducted from salaries in each state that levies professional tax.

## Due dates by state

- **Andhra Pradesh** (monthly): by the 10th of the following month
- **Assam** (monthly): by the last day of the month following the month in which the salary is paid (return in Form III)
- **Bihar** (annual): deducted from the September salary and paid by 15 November; annual return in Form PT-VIII by the end of November. For employees who become liable after September: from the February salary, paid by 15 April
- **Gujarat** (monthly): by the 15th of the following month
- **Jharkhand** (annual tax, paid quarterly): quarterly online in Form JPT-03; the return in Form JPT-04 is generated on payment
- **Karnataka** (monthly): by the 20th of the following month; annual return (Form 5) by 30 May
- **Kerala** (half-yearly): recovered from the August and February salaries and paid to the local body within 10 days of deduction
- **Madhya Pradesh** (monthly): by the 10th of the following month
- **Maharashtra** (monthly): by the 15th of each month from March 2026; annual return by 15 March where the liability is below Rs. 1,00,000
- **Mizoram** (deducted monthly, paid annually): annual payment by 30 June
- **Nagaland** (monthly): paid monthly by challan with the return in Form V; statement of salaries and deductions by 30 April
- **Puducherry** (half-yearly): for each half-year, on the municipality's requisition
- **Punjab** (monthly): by the last day of the following month, or a yearly lump sum by 30 April
- **Sikkim** (deducted monthly, paid quarterly): by 31 July, 31 October, 31 January and 30 April
- **Tamil Nadu** (half-yearly): April to September by 30 September; October to March by 31 March
- **Telangana** (monthly): by the 10th of the following month
- **West Bengal** (monthly): by the 21st of the following month (quarterly where the liability is under Rs. 3,000)

**Manipur and Tripura.** The employer deducts the tax from salaries and pays it for its employees. Persons who enrol themselves, such as professionals and traders, pay once a year by 30 September, or within one month if they enrol after 31 August.

**Meghalaya.** Since Act No. 15 of 2022 the rates are notified by the State Government rather than set out in the Act. The amended section 12(5) charges interest at 12 per cent a year on a registered employer that does not pay by the last day of the month following the quarter, unless at least 90 per cent of the tax due was paid on time.

## The four kinds of cycle

**Monthly.** Most states. The tax deducted from one month's salary is paid by a fixed day of the next month. The day varies: the 10th in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Madhya Pradesh, the 15th in Gujarat and Maharashtra, the 20th in Karnataka, the 21st in West Bengal and the last day of the month in Assam and Punjab.

**Quarterly.** Sikkim and Jharkhand collect quarterly. West Bengal allows quarterly payment where the liability is under Rs. 3,000.

**Half-yearly.** Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Puducherry levy the tax for each half-year (April to September and October to March), collected by the local body. The deduction is usually made once in each half, not every month.

**Annual.** Bihar deducts the whole year's tax from one salary, and Mizoram's annual payment is due by 30 June.

## Building one calendar for a multi-state payroll

1. **List every state of work, not every state of registration.** Professional tax follows the employee's place of work. A company registered in a state with no professional tax still owes it for staff in states that levy it.
2. **Put each due date in the compliance calendar as a separate entry.** Do not round them to one common date. Paying Karnataka on the 10th is early but harmless; paying Andhra Pradesh on the 20th is late.
3. **Mark the non-monthly states clearly.** Bihar's September deduction, Kerala's August and February deductions and Tamil Nadu's 30 September and 31 March dates are easy to miss because nothing happens in the other months.
4. **Track returns as well as payments.** Karnataka's annual return by 30 May, Maharashtra's annual return by 15 March and Bihar's return by the end of November are separate deadlines.
5. **Hold portal access centrally.** Most states now take payment online. Keep the logins on official email addresses and mobile numbers, not with one person.

## What a late payment costs

Each state's Act charges interest on late payment and allows a penalty. Karnataka, for example, charges interest of 1.5 per cent a month on default, plus a penalty. The tax itself is small, so the interest and penalty can easily be larger than the amount that was late.

## Keeping it current

Due dates change less often than slabs, but they do change: Maharashtra moved its date to the 15th from March 2026, and Punjab added a yearly lump-sum option from November 2025. The [professional tax pages by state](/updates/professional-tax) carry the current slab, cycle and due date for each state with a link to the official source, and the [Salary Proration Calculator](/salary-proration) applies each state's rule to part-month salaries.

## Key takeaway

There is no national due date for professional tax. Build the calendar state by state from the place of work, give the half-yearly and annual states their own entries, and track the return dates separately from the payment dates.
