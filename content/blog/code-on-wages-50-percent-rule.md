**Quick answer:** Under Section 2(y) of the Code on Wages, if the components excluded from "wages" (HRA, conveyance, bonus, overtime, employer PF/pension contribution, and a short list of others) add up to more than 50% of an employee's total remuneration, the excess over 50% gets added back into "wages." Most explanations of this rule get one thing wrong: **Special Allowance is not on the excluded list.** If your salary structure uses it as a catch-all, it's already part of wages before the 50% test is even relevant - which usually means your real wage base is higher than the "exactly 50%" answer most calculators give you.

## What the 50% rule actually says

Section 2(y) defines "wages" as basic pay, dearness allowance, and retaining allowance, then lists eleven components excluded from that definition - among them HRA, conveyance allowance, statutory bonus, overtime allowance, commission, employer PF/pension contribution, gratuity, and retrenchment compensation.

The proviso attached to that list is the part that matters operationally: **if these excluded components add up to more than 50% of an employee's total remuneration, the excess over 50% is added back into "wages."** The law doesn't let a company shrink the statutory wage base by structuring pay so allowances do the work basic salary used to do.

## The three tiers - and where Special Allowance actually sits

**Always wages** - Basic pay, dearness allowance, retaining allowance, and any allowance not on the excluded list, including a generic Special Allowance. Counted in full, no test applied.

**Tested against 50%** - HRA, conveyance, statutory bonus, overtime allowance, commission, employer PF/pension contribution, and the other named exclusions under clauses (a)-(i). Excluded unless their total crosses 50% of remuneration - only the excess over 50% is added back.

**Not part of remuneration at all** - Gratuity, retrenchment compensation, other retirement benefits, ex-gratia on termination - clauses (j) and (k). Not tracked for the 50% test in the first place; the proviso only runs across (a)-(i).

The confusion almost always comes from that middle row. Special Allowance sounds like it should belong there - it doesn't. Clause (e), the closest candidate, covers reimbursement for expenses the job specifically entails, not a generic balancing line that mops up whatever's left of CTC. Unless your "Special Allowance" genuinely functions as a reimbursement, it's wages under the *first* tier, not a tested exclusion.

## The most common mistake, in a worked example

Take an employee with total monthly remuneration of Rs 60,000, structured as:

- Basic: Rs 20,000
- HRA: Rs 15,000
- Conveyance: Rs 5,000
- Special Allowance: Rs 15,000
- Bonus (monthly equivalent): Rs 5,000

**The mistake:** treating Special Allowance as one of the excluded components, then running the 50% test on HRA + Conveyance + Special Allowance + Bonus (Rs 40,000 - over half of Rs 60,000), and add-backing the excess to land on a "clean" Rs 30,000 wage base. It's a tidy number, and it's wrong.

**The correct read:** Special Allowance isn't excluded at all - it's wages from the start, alongside Basic.

- Wages baseline = Basic (Rs 20,000) + Special Allowance (Rs 15,000) = **Rs 35,000**
- Components actually tested against 50%: HRA (Rs 15,000) + Conveyance (Rs 5,000) + Bonus (Rs 5,000) = Rs 25,000
- 50% of total remuneration (Rs 60,000) = Rs 30,000
- Rs 25,000 doesn't cross Rs 30,000, so **no add-back applies** - the 50% mechanism never fires

**Recalculated wages: Rs 35,000** - almost 58% of total remuneration, not the "exactly 50%" figure the flawed version produces. The takeaway isn't that the 50% rule doesn't matter here; it's that Special Allowance already did the work before the rule was even needed.

## What this changes - and what it doesn't

**Confirmed to move with the recalculated wage base:**

- **PF contributions** - EPF is calculated on "wages," so a higher recalculated wage base means a higher PF contribution for both employee and employer. This now applies up to the statutory wage ceiling, raised to Rs 25,000 in September 2026 - see our [full explainer on the EPFO ceiling change](/blog/epf-wage-ceiling-25000).
- **ESI contributions** - ESI's own wage definition under the ESI Act is generally broader than the Code on Wages definition, but the underlying principle is the same: contributions reflect actual remuneration, not a structured-down basic salary.

**Commonly cited as affected, but genuinely contested:**

- **Gratuity** - most compliance commentary lists gratuity alongside PF and ESI. But gratuity itself sits in the "not part of remuneration" tier above, and some specialist commentary argues the 50% add-back doesn't apply to gratuity's 15/26 calculation the same way it applies to a monthly accrual like PF. Not settled the way PF's treatment is - worth a direct conversation with your compliance advisor if your gratuity liability is material.
- **Leave encashment** - a Ministry of Labour and Employment FAQ (No. 5, dated 30 December 2025) addresses this directly: leave encashment is not part of the allowance pool tested under Section 2(y). Continues to be governed by your state's Shops & Establishments rules or company policy on a basic + DA basis, separate from this provision.
- **Performance incentives and ESOPs** - the same Ministry FAQ set (No. 3, 30 December 2025) states that performance-based incentives, ESOPs, variable pay, and reimbursement-based payments are not part of wages under this definition. Worth knowing if your comp structure leans on variable pay - it doesn't get pulled into the 50% test pool either way.

**Not affected, despite sometimes being grouped with it:**

- **Overtime pay itself** - overtime allowance is one of the components *counted* when testing whether exclusions cross 50%. It's an input to the calculation, not something the calculation changes. Your overtime rate stays governed by its own rule (commonly basic + DA, at a minimum of twice the normal rate).

## Where enforcement actually stands

The four Labour Codes are in force nationally, but state-level Rules - which govern the practical, on-the-ground application - were still being notified unevenly as state governments worked through their own rule-making. If you operate across multiple states, don't assume uniform applicability; check the notified status in each state you have employees in.

## FAQs

**Is Special Allowance excluded from wages under the Code on Wages?**

Generally no. Section 2(y) lists eleven specific exclusions, and a generic Special Allowance isn't one of them. Unless it genuinely functions as a reimbursement for job-specific expenses, it counts as wages from the start.

**What is the 50% rule under Section 2(y)?**

If the components that *are* named as exclusions (HRA, conveyance, bonus, overtime, commission, employer PF/pension contribution, and others) together exceed 50% of an employee's total remuneration, the amount over 50% is added back into "wages."

**Does the 50% rule mean my wage base is always exactly 50% of CTC?**

No - that's only the outcome when the tested exclusions cross the 50% line by exactly enough to make it so. If a component like Special Allowance is already wages (not tested), your actual wage base can land well above 50%, as in the worked example here.

**Does this rule affect gratuity calculations?**

It's commonly cited as affecting gratuity, but this is genuinely contested among practitioners - check with your compliance advisor if the amount involved is material.

**Does this rule change how overtime is calculated?**

No. Overtime allowance is an input used to test the 50% threshold, not something the 50% rule itself recalculates.

## Key takeaway

The 50% rule doesn't ban high-allowance salary structures - it stops them from shrinking the statutory wage base below half of actual pay. But the rule most people misapply isn't the 50% test itself - it's assuming Special Allowance needs the test in the first place. It usually doesn't: it's wages already. Get that classification right first, and the 50% mechanism only matters for what's left.
